Your podcast is done: **Doing and Feeling**, four episodes, 62 minutes in total, about 15 minutes each, with two synthetic voices. Before rendering, three research passes checked the facts, and a separate reviewer read the scripts; its corrections are in.

**Your two links.** Both are Blaise Agüera y Arcas's *What Is Intelligence?*: the book (MIT Press, Sept 2025, free to read online) and his ~80-minute lecture on it. It's one of the best single overviews of the intelligence side of your question. But it argues a position rather than surveying the field. His claim is that prediction is intelligence and that some AI systems may have a real claim to consciousness. Read it alongside Anil Seth's paper on the biological view, the other side of the argument, published in full last month with 50 replies from other researchers. If you're short on time, watch the lecture, then read the introduction and chapters 6–8.

**What to know, in five lines**
1. Intelligence (doing) and consciousness (feeling) are separate questions. Most confusion comes from merging them.
2. Intelligence is increasingly defined as modeling and prediction, plus how fast you learn something new. It evolved separately in octopuses and in vertebrates.
3. The hard problem asks why any brain processing feels like something. Positions range from "it only seems to" (illusionism) to "feeling is a basic feature of matter" (panpsychism).
4. Science can reliably detect consciousness in humans but can't yet explain how it arises. The first head-to-head test of the two leading theories (2025) dented both.
5. For AI, it mostly comes down to whether the right computation is enough. Serious researchers disagree, and nobody knows.
