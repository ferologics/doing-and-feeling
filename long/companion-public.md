# Doing and Feeling: The Long Version — Companion

October 25, 2026

## How to use this companion

This companion gives the sources behind each of the 14 episodes and where to read further. Links were checked in October 2026.

- **Per episode:** the one argument the episode makes, its key sources, and further reading.
- **Source labels:** links go to the paper or the author's own page where possible. *(secondary)* marks a news report or summary, used when the original was paywalled or unreachable.
- **Errors:** comment on any passage you think is wrong.
- **Where the reading list comes from:** recommendations gathered from replies to Will Depue's October 2026 post on X asking for reading on consciousness ([the thread](https://x.com/willdepue/status/2113823172336505051)). Those the episodes didn't cover are grouped near the end.

## Where to start

Sample several theories before going deep on one. That advice comes from the philosopher Henry Shevlin, now at Google DeepMind: consciousness science is full of people who think they have solved it.

**Shevlin's tasting menu** (one book from each of six authors):

- Stanislas Dehaene, *Consciousness and the Brain* (2014) — global workspace. [LessWrong summary](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/x4n4jcoDP7xh5LWLq/book-summary-consciousness-and-the-brain)
- Steve Fleming, *Know Thyself* (2021) — metacognition.
- Anil Seth, *Being You* (2021) — predictive processing and the beast machine.
- Andy Clark, *The Experience Machine* (2023) — the predictive brain.
- Peter Godfrey-Smith, *Other Minds* (2016) — octopuses and the evolution of minds.
- David Chalmers, *The Conscious Mind* (1996) — the hard problem.

**Or read one target article with its commentaries.** *Behavioral and Brain Sciences* publishes a target article together with dozens of peer commentaries and the author's reply. Shevlin's pick is Anil Seth's "Conscious artificial intelligence and biological naturalism": 50 commentaries and Seth's reply, "The stuff matters" (September 2026). [Open copy](https://sussex.figshare.com/articles/journal_contribution/Conscious_artificial_intelligence_and_biological_naturalism/28649519) · [Journal page](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/journals/behavioral-and-brain-sciences/article/conscious-artificial-intelligence-and-biological-naturalism/C9912A5BE9D806012E3C8B3AF612E39A)

**A short starter sequence** for the core tension in the field:

1. Thomas Nagel, "What Is It Like to Be a Bat?" (1974), about a 30-minute read.
2. Frank Jackson's Mary's room argument (1982).
3. Chalmers, ["Facing Up to the Problem of Consciousness"](https://web.ics.purdue.edu/~drkelly/ChalmersHardProblem1995.pdf) (1995).
4. Daniel Dennett, ["The Illusion of Consciousness"](https://www.ted.com/talks/dan_dennett_the_illusion_of_consciousness) (TED talk).

## Part I — Doing

## Episode 1 — Two Questions

**Argument:** the general factor, g, is one of psychology's best-replicated and most predictive findings, but its causes, what heritability means, and the field's eugenic history are less settled than fans or critics claim.

**Key sources**

- The doing/feeling distinction: Seth, Block & Tononi, ["Three reasons why AI is not conscious and perhaps never will be"](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-03313-7), *Nature* 658:866–868 (October 2026).
- Spearman 1904, ["General Intelligence," Objectively Determined and Measured](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/Spearman/). The three-stratum and CHC models: [overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cattell%E2%80%93Horn%E2%80%93Carroll_theory) *(secondary)*.
- Why abilities correlate, four rival explanations: [van der Maas et al. 2006, mutualism](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.113.4.842); [Kovács & Conway 2016, process overlap](https://www.gwern.net/doc/iq/2016-kovacs.pdf); [Borsboom, Mellenbergh & van Heerden 2003, what a factor model licenses](https://dare.uva.nl/id/20a1ec01-07fd-4bb9-bd56-700b9ba5024f).
- History: [Buck v. Bell (1927)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/274/200/); the dispute over testing and the 1924 Immigration Act, [Snyderman & Herrnstein 1983](https://www.gwern.net/doc/iq/1983-snyderman.pdf).
- Prediction: [Sackett et al. 2024 on job performance](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/a-contemporary-look-at-the-relationship-between-general-cognitive/) and the [Oh et al. dispute](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/correcting-for-range-restriction-in-meta-analysis-a-reply-to-oh-e/); [Calvin et al. 2011 on mortality](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/55194).
- Heritability: [Plomin & Deary 2015](https://www.research.ed.ac.uk/en/publications/genetics-and-intelligence-differences-five-special-findings/); [Tucker-Drob & Bates 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4749462); [Figlio et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5754768/); [Okbay et al. 2022, polygenic prediction within families](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/polygenic-prediction-of-educational-attainment-within-and-between/).
- The Flynn effect: [Wicherts et al. 2004 on measurement invariance](https://www.gwern.net/doc/iq/2004-wicherts.pdf); [Bratsberg & Rogeberg 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29891660/).
- Group differences: [APA task force 1996](https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.51.2.77); [Nisbett et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0026699); the minority hereditarian case, [Rushton & Jensen 2005](https://www1.udel.edu/educ/gottfredson/30years/Rushton-Jensen30years.pdf).
- National IQ data: [Wicherts, Dolan & van der Maas 2010](https://research.tilburguniversity.edu/en/publications/a-systematic-literature-review-of-the-average-iq-of-sub-saharan-a/); [retractions of papers using the Lynn dataset](https://retractionwatch.com/2025/11/25/meet-the-researcher-aiming-to-halt-use-of-fundamentally-flawed-database-linking-iq-and-nationality) *(secondary)*.

**Further reading:** Stephen Jay Gould's *The Mismeasure of Man* and Ian Deary's *Intelligence: A Very Short Introduction* read well as a pair, one critic and one defender of the field.

## Episode 2 — Minds in Other Bodies

**Argument:** intelligence evolved several times in very different bodies, so it is a family of solutions rather than a ladder, and science errs in both directions, seeing too much (Clever Hans) and too little (what Frans de Waal calls anthropodenial).

**Key sources**

- Crows: [Weir, Chappell & Kacelnik 2002, Betty's hook](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1073433); [Rutz et al. 2016, wild-caught crows bending twigs](https://news.st-andrews.ac.uk/archive/tool-bending-birds-prompt-clever-crow-rethink/) *(secondary)*; [Kabadayi & Osvath 2017, ravens planning](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aam8138); [Clayton & Dickinson 1998, scrub-jay memory](https://doi.org/10.1038/26216); [Emery & Clayton 2001, re-caching](https://doi.org/10.1038/35106560).
- Bird brains: [Olkowicz et al. 2016, neuron counts](https://news.vanderbilt.edu/2016/06/13/study-gives-new-meaning-to-the-term-bird-brain/) *(secondary)*; [Stacho et al. 2020, cortex-like circuits](https://www.gwern.net/doc/psychology/animal/bird/neuroscience/2020-stacho.pdf); the February 2025 *Science* papers on convergent evolution, [summary](https://www.ikerbasque.net/en/news/birds-have-developed-complex-brains-independently-mammals) *(secondary)*.
- Octopuses and cuttlefish: [Albertin et al. 2015, the octopus genome](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature14668); [Amodio et al. 2019 on why cephalopods got clever](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tree.2018.10.010); [Schnell et al. 2021, the cuttlefish marshmallow test](https://phys.org/news/2021-03-quick-learning-cuttlefish-marshmallow.html) *(secondary)*.
- Bees: [Chittka & Niven 2009, "Are bigger brains better?"](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2009.08.023); [Alem et al. 2016, string pulling](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.1002564); [Bridges et al. 2024, culture in bumblebees](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/209019/16/s41586-024-07126-4.pdf); [Dona et al. 2022, play](https://www.sci.news/biology/bumblebee-play-11342.html) *(secondary)*.
- A general factor in animals: [Reader, Hager & Laland 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3049098); [Burkart, Schubiger & van Schaik 2017](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X16000959); [Herrmann et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1146282); [MacLean et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1323533111).
- Basal cognition and plants: [Levin 2022, TAME](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.10346); [Tero et al. 2010, slime mold and the Tokyo rail network](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1177894); [Gagliano et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep38427) and [Markel's 2020 failed replication](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.57614); [Taiz et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tplants.2019.05.008).
- Groups: [Woolley et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1193147); [Riedl et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8166150).

**Further reading:** Peter Godfrey-Smith, *Other Minds* (2016); Frans de Waal, *Are We Smart Enough to Know How Smart Animals Are?* (2016); Lars Chittka, *The Mind of a Bee* (2022).

## Episode 3 — Intelligence, Formally

**Argument:** the formal definitions tie intelligence to prediction and compression, but every definition builds in priors, so machine scores stay contested and depend on how the test is run.

**Key sources**

- [The Hutter Prize](https://hutter1.net/prize/index.htm) for compressing Wikipedia, and [Delétang et al. 2024, "Language Modeling Is Compression"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.10668).
- Turing 1950, ["Computing Machinery and Intelligence"](https://doi.org/10.1093/mind/LIX.236.433); [Jones & Bergen 2025, a three-party Turing test](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.23674).
- Shannon [1948](https://doi.org/10.1002/j.1538-7305.1948.tb01338.x) and [1951, prediction and the entropy of English](https://doi.org/10.1002/j.1538-7305.1951.tb01366.x).
- Legg & Hutter, [a collection of \~70 definitions](https://arxiv.org/abs/0706.3639) and [universal intelligence](https://arxiv.org/abs/0712.3329); [Veness et al. 2011, an approximation of AIXI](https://jair.org/index.php/jair/article/view/10685); [Leike & Hutter 2015, why the definition is subjective](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v40/Leike15.html).
- No free lunch: [Wolpert & Macready 1997](https://doi.org/10.1109/4235.585893).
- Chollet 2019, ["On the Measure of Intelligence"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.01547). The ARC results: [o3](https://arcprize.org/blog/oai-o3-pub-breakthrough); [ARC Prize 2025](https://arcprize.org/blog/arc-prize-2025-results-analysis); [the Tiny Recursive Model](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.04871v1); [Claude Opus 5](https://arcprize.org/results/anthropic-claude-opus-5) (Anthropic, the series' maker); [GPT-6 Astra under two harnesses](https://arcprize.org/blog/astra).
- Evaluation science: [Zhang et al. 2024, GSM1k contamination check](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00332v1); [Bean et al. 2025, "Measuring What Matters"](https://www.oii.ox.ac.uk/study-identifies-weaknesses-in-how-ai-systems-are-evaluated/) *(secondary)*.
- Defining AGI: [Hendrycks et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.18212); METR's [time horizons](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14499) and [current estimates](https://metr.org/time-horizons/).
- A g factor in machines: [Ilić 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11616); [Krakauer 2026, "The Rise and Fall of G in AGI"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09911).

**Further reading:** Chollet's paper above is the best single essay on what an intelligence test should measure; Marcus Hutter's *Universal Artificial Intelligence* (2005) is the rigorous treatment.

## Episode 4 — Life as Computation

**Argument:** the strongest case that life, intelligence and computation lie on one continuum, built on Blaise Agüera y Arcas's *What Is Intelligence?*, then tested against the objection that, on a loose enough definition, everything computes.

**Key sources**

- Agüera y Arcas, [*What Is Intelligence?*](https://whatisintelligence.antikythera.org/) (MIT Press, 2025; free online) and [the publisher's page](https://mitpressbookstore.mit.edu/book/9780262049955).
- Von Neumann's [universal constructor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Von_Neumann_universal_constructor) *(secondary)*; [Lenski et al. 2003, evolving complex functions in Avida](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature01568).
- [Agüera y Arcas et al. 2024, "Computational Life"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.19108): self-replicators arising from random programs.
- Symbiogenesis: [Lynn Margulis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lynn_Margulis) *(secondary)*; John Maynard Smith & Eörs Szathmáry, *The Major Transitions in Evolution* (1995).
- The physics of prediction: [Still et al. 2012, "Thermodynamics of Prediction"](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.109.120604); assembly theory, [Sharma et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06600-9), and the [critique by Abrahão et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06629).
- Regulation and models: [Conant & Ashby 1970, the good regulator theorem](https://doi.org/10.1080/00207727008920220); [Richens et al. 2025, "General agents contain world models"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01622).
- Social intelligence: [Nicholas Humphrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_Humphrey) *(secondary)*; [Dunbar's number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunbar%27s_number) *(secondary)* and its diet-based challenger, [DeCasien et al. 2017](https://www.sci.news/othersciences/anthropology/social-brain-hypothesis-challenged-04738.html) *(secondary)*.
- The "everything computes" objection and replies: [Chalmers 1996, on whether a rock implements every automaton](https://consc.net/papers/rock.html); [Farrell, Gopnik, Shalizi & Evans 2025, AI as a cultural and social technology](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adt9819).
- Reviews of the book: [Patrick House, *Los Angeles Review of Books*](https://lareviewofbooks.org/article/what-isnt-intelligence/).

**Further reading:** Erwin Schrödinger's *What Is Life?* (1944), especially its closing chapter; Sara Walker, *Life as No One Knows It* (2024).

## Episode 5 — The Predictive Brain

**Argument:** the brain is plausibly a prediction machine, but the evidence is messier than the slogans, and the free energy principle buys its generality at the cost of testability.

**Key sources**

- The hollow-mask illusion in schizophrenia: [Dima et al. 2009](https://www.news-medical.net/news/2009/04/06/48010.aspx) *(secondary)*.
- Bayesian perception: [Ernst & Banks 2002](https://doi.org/10.1038/415429a); the counter-case, [Rahnev & Denison 2018, "Suboptimality in perceptual decision making"](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X18000936) (a target article with commentaries).
- Predictive coding: [Rao & Ballard 1999](https://doi.org/10.1038/4580); [Summerfield et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.2163); [Zheng & Meister 2024, "The Unbearable Slowness of Being"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.10234).
- Recent tests: the multi-species preprint ["Adaptation, not prediction"](https://www.allenneuraldynamics.org/tools/adaptation-not-prediction-drives-neuronal-spiking-responses-in-mammalian-sensory-cortex); [Peterka et al., context in V1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12803279); the [International Brain Laboratory brain-wide map](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/news/2025/sep/complete-brain-activity-map-revealed-first-time) *(secondary)*.
- The free energy principle: Parr, Pezzulo & Friston, [*Active Inference*](https://direct.mit.edu/books/oa-monograph/5299/Active-InferenceThe-Free-Energy-Principle-in-Mind) (open access); [Friston, Thornton & Clark 2012 on the dark-room problem](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2012.00130); the critique ["The Emperor's New Markov Blankets"](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X21002351).
- Dopamine: [Schultz, Dayan & Montague 1997](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.275.5306.1593); [Dabney et al. 2020, distributional coding](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-1924-6); [Jeong et al. 2022, a retrospective alternative](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abq6740).
- Cognitive maps: [the 2014 Nobel Prize](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/medicine/2014/press-release/); [Stachenfeld, Botvinick & Gershman 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.4650); [Constantinescu, O'Reilly & Behrens 2016](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aaf0941); [Whittington et al. 2020, the Tolman-Eichenbaum Machine](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7707106/).

**Further reading:** Andy Clark, *The Experience Machine* (2023), the most readable account; Parr, Pezzulo & Friston's *Active Inference* for the formal version; Artem Kirsanov's video ["Can We Build an Artificial Hippocampus?"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cufOEzoVMVA) on cognitive maps and their link to transformers.

## Part II — Feeling

## Episode 7 — The Hard Problem

**Argument:** why anything is felt is a different question from what a system can do, and the classic arguments for that are stronger than their caricatures, but so are the replies.

**Key sources**

- Leibniz's mill, [*Monadology* §17](https://hume.ucdavis.edu/kant/MONADOLO.HTM) (1714); du Bois-Reymond's [ignorabimus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ignoramus_et_ignorabimus) *(secondary)*; Ryle's [category mistake](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/category-mistakes/).
- Thomas Nagel, ["What Is It Like to Be a Bat?"](https://www.jstor.org/stable/2183914) (1974); Joseph Levine, "Materialism and Qualia: The Explanatory Gap" (1983).
- David Chalmers, ["Facing Up to the Problem of Consciousness"](https://web.ics.purdue.edu/~drkelly/ChalmersHardProblem1995.pdf) (1995) and ["Consciousness and its Place in Nature"](https://consc.net/papers/nature.html) (2003), the type A–F map.
- Zombies and conceivability: [Stanford Encyclopedia, "Zombies"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/zombies/). Mary's room and its replies: [Stanford Encyclopedia, "Qualia: The Knowledge Argument"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/qualia-knowledge/).
- Access versus phenomenal consciousness, and overflow: Ned Block 1995 and 2007, both in *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*; the generic-letters reply, [Ian Phillips on iconic memory](https://www.ianbphillips.com/files/papers/perception-and-iconic-memory.pdf).
- Wittgenstein, [*The Blue Book*](https://www.wittgensteinproject.org/w/index.php/Blue_Book) and *Philosophical Investigations* §§293 and 412.
- Chalmers 2018, ["The Meta-Problem of Consciousness"](https://philpapers.org/archive/chatmo-32.pdf).
- What philosophers think: the [2020 PhilPapers survey](https://survey2020.philpeople.org/), [summarized here](https://selfawarepatterns.com/2021/11/07/what-philosophers-believe-2020-edition/) *(secondary)*.

**Further reading:** the starter sequence at the top of this companion; then Chalmers's *The Conscious Mind* (1996).

## Episode 8 — The Case for Illusion

**Argument:** illusionism, the view that experience only seems to have special private qualities, deserves a full-strength hearing: it rests on good evidence that introspection misleads, and it turns the hard problem into a research program, though its hardest objections still stand.

**Key sources**

- The color experiment: [Cohen, Botch & Robertson 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7306755).
- Keith Frankish, ["Illusionism as a Theory of Consciousness"](https://www.keithfrankish.com/illusionism/) (2016), and the [collected symposium](https://philpapers.org/rec/FRAIAA-4).
- Daniel Dennett, ["Quining Qualia"](https://web.ics.purdue.edu/~drkelly/DennettQuiningQualia1988.pdf) (1988), *Consciousness Explained* (1991), and the [TED talk](https://www.ted.com/talks/dan_dennett_the_illusion_of_consciousness).
- Introspection's track record: Nisbett & Wilson 1977, "Telling More Than We Can Know"; [Schwitzgebel 2008](https://doi.org/10.1215/00318108-2007-037); [Hurlburt and colleagues on inner speech](https://www.hurlburt.faculty.unlv.edu/hurlburt-heavey-kelsey-2013-inner-speaking.pdf); [Zeman et al. 2015 on aphantasia](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cortex.2015.05.019).
- Mechanisms: Graziano's [reply to Frankish](https://grazianolab.princeton.edu/document/121) and [attention schema agent](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/the-attention-schema-theory-in-a-neural-network-agent-controlling/); Humphrey's ["Redder than Red"](https://www.ingentaconnect.com/contentone/imp/jcs/2016/00000023/f0020011/art00009); Hinton in [the Globe and Mail](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/business/article-geoffrey-hinton-artificial-intelligence-machines-feelings); Agüera y Arcas, [chapter 6](https://whatisintelligence.antikythera.org/chapter-06/).
- The objections: Galen Strawson, ["The Consciousness Deniers"](https://philosophytalk.org/blog/consciousness-deniers/) *(secondary)*, and [the exchange with Dennett](https://www.nybooks.com/online/2018/04/03/magic-illusions-and-zombies-an-exchange/); Philip Goff, *Galileo's Error* (2019).
- Joe Carlsmith, ["Grokking Illusionism"](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/SJjaWcMKtcdM3FW9u) (2021).
- Fiction: Peter Watts, *Blindsight* (2006), [with its endnotes](https://rifters.com/real/shorts/PeterWatts_Blindsight_Endnotes.pdf).

## The edges of the map

These ideas, covered in the closing segment of episode 9, are popular and often recommended, but sit outside mainstream science or have little supporting evidence so far. The test for each is the same as for the mainstream: a prediction someone could check.

| Idea | Claim | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Federico Faggin and Giacomo Mauro D'Ariano, *Irreducible* (2024); [their paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.06580) | Consciousness and free will are fundamental; experience belongs to quantum systems in pure states, so classical computers can never be conscious | Theoretical proposal; experiments sketched, none carried out |
| Michael Johnson, symmetry theory of valence, [*Principia Qualia*](https://opentheory.net/PrincipiaQualia.pdf) (2016); [primer](https://www.greaterwrong.com/posts/dfrQbbv6Np7GuWjDR/a-primer-on-the-symmetry-theory-of-valence) | How good or bad an experience feels is encoded in the symmetry of its mathematical description | Conjecture; brain-imaging tests proposed, no independent confirmation |
| David Pearce, [non-materialist physicalism](https://www.hedweb.com/physicalism/) | The physical is intrinsically experiential; the unity of experience comes from very brief quantum coherence in neurons | Speculative; the proposed test is not yet feasible |
| Donald Hoffman, *The Case Against Reality* (2019); [the interface theory](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-015-0890-8) | Perception is tuned to fitness, not truth; reality is a network of conscious agents | The simulations are published and debated; the conscious-agent theory has no confirmed predictions |
| Thomas Campbell, *My Big TOE* | Physical reality is a virtual reality within a larger consciousness system | Double-slit tests proposed and [one begun in 2024](https://ispr.info/2024/08/16/former-nasa-scientist-doing-experiment-to-prove-we-live-in-a-simulation/) *(secondary)*; no peer-reviewed results |
| Itzhak Bentov, *Stalking the Wild Pendulum* (1977) | Consciousness as vibration and oscillation underlying matter | Popular and speculative; never developed into a testable theory |
| Ken Wilber, integral theory | Quadrants and stages mapping consciousness, culture and contemplative development | A philosophical and spiritual synthesis, not an empirical theory |
| Wolfgang Pauli and Carl Jung, their correspondence | Mind and matter as two aspects of one reality; synchronicity | A serious subject for historians and philosophers, not an empirical theory |
| Julian Jaynes, *The Origin of Consciousness in the Breakdown of the Bicameral Mind* (1976); [overview](https://www.julianjaynes.org/about/about-jaynes-theory/overview/) | Introspective consciousness arose about 3,000 years ago; earlier people heard one hemisphere's commands as the voices of gods | Admired as a provocation; [historical claims widely disputed](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/the-bicameral-mind-30-years-on-a-critical-reappraisal-of-julian-j/), though its emphasis on language in shaping introspection has defenders |

- Gilbert Ryle, *The Concept of Mind* (1949); John Haugeland, "Mind Embodied and Embedded"; Martin Heidegger, *Being and Time*, the introduction and most of division one.
- Friedrich Nietzsche, *The Gay Science* §354, on consciousness as late and linguistic.
- Daniel Dennett and Douglas Hofstadter (eds.), *The Mind's I* (1981), including Hofstadter's ["Prelude… Ant Fugue"](https://bert.stuy.edu/pbrooks/fall2014/materials/HumanReasoning/Hofstadter-PreludeAntFugue.pdf); *Mind Design III* (MIT Press).
- On illusionism: [a lecture course on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLhgvALi0LQGXIA7cKNmGNTiQ7dpS-7dLw).

**Life, biology and machines**

- Humberto Maturana and Francisco Varela, *The Tree of Knowledge* (1987).
- Slime Mold Time Mold, [*The Mind in the Wheel*](https://slimemoldtimemold.com/2025/02/06/the-mind-in-the-wheel-prologue-everybody-wants-a-rock/) (2025).
- Michael Timothy Bennett, *How to Build Conscious Machines* (thesis) and [talk](https://youtu.be/boTAgdpme5s).
- Geoffrey Hinton, ["I Don't Believe in Consciousness"](https://youtu.be/f-08IkK0UxM) (talk, 2017); David Deutsch, ["How Quantum Theory Leads to Conscious AI"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iuTn2vgkayA) (interview).
- Max Tegmark, *Life 3.0* (2017); Ray Kurzweil, *The Singularity Is Nearer* (2024).
- Reading lists elsewhere: [smoothbrains.net](https://smoothbrains.net/posts/2025-10-18-three-year-retrospective.html#recommended-reading) and ["A Paradigm for AI Consciousness"](https://www.theseedsofscience.pub/p/a-paradigm-for-ai-consciousness).
- Sebastian Cochinescu, [*Perceived AGI*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15883) (2026), a preprint on how expressed attitudes shape the impression of a mind; untested so far.

**Meditation and contemplative traditions**

- Ruben Laukkonen's essay ["A beautiful loop"](https://rubenlaukkonen.substack.com/p/a-beautiful-loop), and talks by Kathryn Devaney, Laukkonen and Shamil Chandaria on advanced meditation.
- Nisargadatta Maharaj, *I Am That*; the *Sankhya Karika*; [a Buddhist-inspired perspective](https://guustaaf.substack.com/p/a-buddhist-inspired-perspective-on).

**Consciousness-first and Qualia Research Institute writing**

- Bernardo Kastrup, *Why Materialism Is Baloney* (2014); the Essentia Foundation's video channel.
- Michael Johnson, ["Against functionalism"](https://opentheory.net/2017/07/why-i-think-the-foundational-research-institute-should-rethink-its-approach/); Andrés Gómez Emilsson's [Qualia Computing](https://qualiacomputing.com/).
- Federico Faggin, ["Consciousness Isn't In Your Body"](https://youtu.be/LGXr-yGPWLY) (interview).

**Why it matters**

- Viktor Frankl, *Man's Search for Meaning* (1946).
